02 · What I'm brought in to do

Three pivots. Most programmes are wrestling with one of them.

AI programmes rarely fail on technology. They stall at a transition nobody owns. These are the three I'm called in for. They run roughly in order, though most organisations are working on two at once, and the second is where the majority are sitting right now.

Pivot 01
Pilot→Production

Make it real, in front of customers

The demo worked. Production requires answering what happens when the model is wrong in front of a customer, who approves that, and what the fallback is. Those are decisions, not engineering tasks, which is why they get deferred.

The tell: an eighteen-month-old pilot everyone still calls promising.
Pivot 02
Initial→Industrialised

Make the second one cheaper than the first

One use case in production is an achievement. Twenty is a capability. That needs shared foundations, governed data, and a delivery pattern others can follow, rather than a hero team rebuilding from scratch each time.

The tell: every new use case costs what the first one did.
Pivot 03
Managed→Resilient

Make it survive without me, and without you

Three shifts at once: it holds up when models drift or vendors change underneath it, it can be defended to a regulator with evidence rather than assurances, and your own team runs it confidently after I leave.

The tell: nobody can explain last Tuesday's decision.

See the argument, not just read it

The idea above, rendered as something you can feel. Noise resolves into order only where attention lands. That is the whole thesis, happening under your cursor.

How I work

Architecture is a stance, not a diagram.

"Anyone can wire up an intelligent system now. The hard part is sensibility: knowing which problem deserves the intelligence, what the output means in context, and when to overrule a confident machine. That judgment is the architecture. The diagram is just its residue."

After twenty-five years across regulated industries, the pattern holds. The platforms that lasted were the ones where someone supplied judgment the models could not, the sensibility to frame the right question before engineering the right answer.

  1. 01

    Frame the right problem first

    Sensibility starts before the model. The costliest mistakes are intelligent answers to the wrong question.

  2. 02

    Engineer context into the system

    Semantic layers, governance, lineage. The infrastructure that lets a model know what its output actually means.

  3. 03

    Then engineer for excellence

    Kafka, Lakehouse, RAG, agentic AI. The technology earns its place once the judgment layer is set.

  4. 04

    Keep a human in the override seat

    Design the system so judgment can intervene. Sensibility at machine speed still needs a person who can say "stop."

How we might work together

Three ways we can work together.

Each has a clear shape and an honest end point, and leaves behind something your team owns and understands. None of them is an open-ended transformation programme.

01 · Foundations4 to 8 weeks

AI data readiness and semantic core

When this helpsSeveral AI pilots are giving your leadership different answers to the same question.

Together we write down the business meaning behind the 30 to 50 numbers your organisation actually runs on: what each one means, where it comes from, who looks after it, and the traps people have learned to step around. Aligned to industry standards where they help, such as BIAN or FHIR. Then we check it honestly, with a before-and-after accuracy test on your real questions, not a benchmark.

What stays with your teamA governed semantic model, a scored readiness map, and a 90-day plan for what to improve next.
02 · Reliability3 to 6 weeks

Agent production readiness and reliability

When this helpsYour agent shines in the demo and falters in production.

I sit with your own experts to turn their knowledge into a test set, run each case repeatedly, and share plainly how often the agent is right and how it fails. Then we look at the design around it together: fixed workflow or agent, retrieval permissions, document preparation, decision record retention, running cost.

What stays with your teamA reliability number the board can trust, and a design your team feels ready to build.
03 · Direction4 to 8 weeks

Data-to-decision architecture and operating model

When this helpsYour lakehouse, BI, AI and automation investments grew up separately and no longer pull in the same direction.

We shape the target architecture together, from source systems through the semantic layer to analytics, agents and action, agree how people and agentic AI will work alongside each other, and pace the journey as the three pivots: pilot to production, initial to industrialised, managed to resilient.

What stays with your teamA reference architecture, the standards that hold it together, and a handover plan so your team runs it confidently on its own.

Where I will point you to someone better

Security testing of agent tools and MCP servers deserves a dedicated specialist, and that is not me. If it is what you need, I will tell you honestly and introduce you to someone I trust, rather than stretching my own experience to cover it.
