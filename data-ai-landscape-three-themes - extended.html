A1 · The Data Stack, Component by Component

Five platforms, decomposed. The ecosystem is not the vendor — it is the layer.

Treating Google Cloud or AWS as a single ecosystem is the mistake that makes partner strategy incoherent. BigQuery, Dataproc and Dataflow are data; Vertex AI is not. Redshift and Glue are data; Bedrock is not. Each layer has its own buyer, its own competitive set, and — critically — its own partner specialization. Click any layer for detail.

The three structural facts across all five

One. Every platform converged on the same governance-plus-conversational shape this cycle: Unity Catalog and Genie, Dataplex and Gemini Enterprise, Purview and Copilot, Horizon and Cortex Analyst. The catalog became the control plane and the natural-language surface sits on top of it. Two. Interoperability is now the default rather than the exception — OneLake Shortcuts reach into S3 and Google Cloud Storage, Snowflake operates on OneLake data through Iceberg and Parquet, and over 70% of enterprises run hybrid or multi-cloud data environments. Single-platform expertise is worth less than it was. Three. Distribution is uneven in a way that should shape which badge you chase first: roughly 70% of the Fortune 500 already have Fabric access through existing Microsoft contracts, which is a larger installed footprint than any competitor can claim.

The wedge, stated precisely

Independent assessment of Databricks Genie names exactly four weaknesses: sources outside Databricks sit outside the room, each business audience needs its own curated room, quality degrades as questions drift from saved examples, and the evidence trail is generated SQL plus result rather than a separated planner, executor and verifier that some audit postures require.

Read that as a services brief someone else wrote for you. Curation per audience is semantic modelling. Drift is evaluation engineering. Audit separation is governance. Cross-source reach is ontology. Four gaps, four pillars, and none of them is something the platform vendor intends to close.

The equivalent on Snowflake is different in shape but identical in kind: Cortex Analyst is API-first, which means the semantic model and the embedding into Slack, Teams or a process app is the customer's problem, not Snowflake's.

A5 · Analytics Deal Shape

Predictive and prescriptive dissolved as a brand while the work continued.

Two independent pieces of evidence. B1 · The AI Stack, Component by Component

Every data platform now has an AI layer, and every hyperscaler has a separate agentic layer.

Vertex AI, Bedrock and AgentCore, Azure AI Foundry, Mosaic AI and Agent Bricks, Cortex AI. These are distinct products with distinct partner designations, not features of the data platform. The most expensive architecture mistake reported in this market — choosing between AgentCore and Q Developer — is a mistake about which layer you are working in.

The pattern across all five AI layers

Each platform has split its AI stack into three separable things: a model layer (Bedrock, Vertex AI, Mosaic AI, Azure AI Foundry, Cortex AI), a retrieval layer (Bedrock Knowledge Bases, Databricks Vector Search, Cortex AISQL over unstructured data), and an agentic runtime (AgentCore with Runtime, Memory, Identity, Tools, Browser, Code Interpreter and Observability; Agent Bricks; Gemini Enterprise Agent Gallery). The model layer is commoditising fastest because the same foundation models appear across all of them — Bedrock alone serves Anthropic Claude, Meta Llama, Mistral and Amazon Nova through one API. The retrieval and grounding layer is where the differentiation moved, and it is the layer that depends on the semantic work in Theme One.

B2 · Frontier and Model Vendor Ecosystems

The model vendors opened services channels in 2026, and they did not open them the same way.

Within four months both frontier labs and the major hyperscalers stood up formal services partner programmes with named funding. The stated logic is identical everywhere: model capability is no longer the constraint on enterprise value, deployment is. What differs commercially is whether the door is open.

The single most actionable finding in this analysis

Anthropic and OpenAI made opposite architectural choices. Anthropic's Claude Partner Network launched 12 March 2026 as an open network, free to join, with a $100 million commitment — over 40,000 firms applied and over 10,000 consultants certified within three months, and the Services Track publishes exact, checkable requirements. OpenAI launched 14 June 2026 with more money at $150 million and a bigger target at 300,000 certified consultants, but its top structure is a named-partner model around Accenture, BCG, McKinsey, Bain and PwC with no public mid-market application pathway found.

One ecosystem has a published ladder you can start climbing this quarter. The other has a guest list. The Partner Hub doubles as a buyer-facing directory ranking firms by certified headcount and deployment history, which means early position compounds — and it will crowd as the large integrators finish certifying.

What the hyperscalers did

Google Cloud committed $750 million at Cloud Next '26 across a 120,000-member ecosystem, embedding its own forward deployed engineers alongside Accenture, Capgemini, Cognizant, Deloitte, Devoteam, HCLTech and TCS, and giving Accenture, Bain, BCG, Deloitte and McKinsey early access to Gemini models. Cognizant was named Diamond partner and stood up a dedicated Gemini Enterprise practice supplying its own FDEs. AWS expanded its AI Competency with three Agentic AI categories and 60 launch partners, calling it its largest specialization launch to date, with automated validation cutting application time by up to 70%.

The detail that proves smaller firms get in

Google named AI-native services firms — Distyl.ai, Tribe.ai, Tryolabs and Artefact — as launching dedicated Gemini Enterprise practices with sandbox credits and upskilling support. These are not global integrators. That is documented precedent that the $750 million fund is not exclusively a large-partner instrument, and it is directly citable in a partner conversation. AWS's automated validation is the same signal in different form: a deliberate lowering of the barrier while the category is early.

C1 · Large Deal Anatomy

End-to-end solution-led is what the market calls a large deal, and it has a specific shape.

Above roughly $25M, data and AI stops being a deal and becomes a workstream inside someone else's programme. The AI framing belongs to the prime. Understanding which band you are actually competing in determines everything downstream — offering structure, pricing construct, partner strategy and hiring.

What a large deal is made of

The flagship AI-led win of the window was described by the vendor itself as global end-to-end managed services encompassing infrastructure, applications, data and global connectivity. Not an AI deal. A managed services deal with an AI rationale. Capgemini's US$3.3B WNS acquisition is the same bet structurally: business process services as the showcase for agentic AI, which is AI as the reason for a BPO body of work.

The attachment design constraint

To participate at $25M+, each pillar must be independently scopeable, priced on a unit the prime can pass through, and must interface with whatever platform estate the prime has chosen — Databricks, Snowflake, Fabric or a client captive's own stack. The test is simple: could a prime buy your ontology pillar alone, with a documented interface, without you touching the rest of the estate? If not, you cannot attach.

C2 · Why Combined Wins

Both single-theme motions have a published failure problem. The combined motion is where the evidence turns.

Pure data modernisation is a price-compressed contest with converging automation claims nobody audits. Pure AI has roughly 95% of generative pilots showing no measurable P&L impact and over 40% of agentic projects forecast for cancellation. The argument for combining is structural rather than rhetorical: the documented cause of AI failure is the data and governance layer, so the firm that sells both is selling the fix and the thing it fixes as one scope.

The structural evidence

Everest moved its specialist assessment from "Analytics and AI" to "Data and AI" — the analyst frame is combined, not split. The structural argument is strong and the direct evidence does not exist. Test it against your own win-loss data before building a business case on it.

C3 · The Semantic Bet, Stress-Tested

The category is thin partly because it is hard, not only because nobody thought of it.

This section exists because an independent check ran against my own recommendation rather than for it. The evidence does not overturn the semantic position, but it materially changes what a realistic business case looks like and what should be promised to a client.

Production track record

The enterprise knowledge graph market is reported at roughly US$3.47 billion in 2026 growing at about 21.3% CAGR, but industry surveys suggest fewer than 15% of enterprises have moved knowledge graph projects beyond pilot, with the majority stalling on ontology design, entity resolution accuracy or query performance. A category with a 15% production rate does not have a marketing problem. It has an execution problem, and execution problems are where services firms earn money — but only if they price for the difficulty.

The three failure modes, named

Semantic governance is a running cost, not a project cost. Maintaining consistent ontologies across departments reportedly needs roughly one dedicated semantic steward per 50 to 100 entity types, ongoing. Entity resolution degrades in production. Matching that achieves 95% accuracy on test data routinely falls to 70 to 80% against production data carrying historical variations and systematic source errors, and those errors propagate silently until they surface in business outcomes months later. Integration effort compounds. Organisations scope on the first two or three source connections and then hit disproportionate complexity at sources five through ten.

What this changes about the recommendation

It does not kill the semantic position. It sharpens it in three ways. First, the annuity is real: if governance genuinely requires a steward per 50 to 100 entity types, an ontology engagement has a managed-service tail rather than being a one-off build, which is a better commercial shape than a project. Second, entity resolution degradation is an argument for the evaluation engineering capability rather than against the semantic one — the two have to be sold together or the first fails silently. Third, the compounding integration cost means fixed-price ontology work beyond three source systems is a losing construct; price per source connected, not per project.

And the risk it exposes

A firm entering this category on the basis that it is uncrowded, without pricing for a 15% production rate, is entering a category where most engagements stall. The differentiated position is not "we have a semantic layer." It is "we have a method for the three things that kill these projects." That is a harder claim to make and a much harder one to copy.

Source note

The production and cost figures come from vendor-published buyer guides in the knowledge graph category, which have a commercial interest in describing the problem as hard. Treat the direction as credible and the specific numbers as indicative. They are corroborated in shape by the independent finding that data preparation consumes 60 to 75% of agentic project effort, and by Gartner attributing the majority of AI project failure to data rather than models.

C4 · Verticalisation

Analyst-recognised, not marketing taxonomy. The premium claim is a different matter.

The test was whether vertical assets appear in hard artifacts or only on websites. They appear. The strongest signal is that analyst firms now run separate industry assessments rather than treating industry as a criterion inside a horizontal one — Everest runs a 30-provider Life Sciences AI and Analytics PEAK alongside a 27-provider horizontal AI PEAK. Databricks runs Insurance, Healthcare, Retail and CPG, and Energy and Utilities as distinct Partner of the Year categories, with named Brickbuilder solutions behind them such as Slalom's Insurance 360.

Where the defensible version sits

There is a real difference between a vertical solution built on proprietary industry logic and one built to a published industry standard. The first is a differentiated asset the client must take on trust. The second is interoperable, auditable, and maps to a model the client's regulator and counterparties already recognise. In regulated industries the second is worth materially more because it converts a vendor claim into a conformance statement. That is the distinction between a vertical accelerator and a vertical ontology.

The honest limit

Analyst recognition proves the market segmented by industry. It does not prove verticalisation commands a price premium. No public data was found showing higher margins, win rates or pricing for vertical versus horizontal data and AI work. Treat the premium as plausible and unevidenced.

Where outcome pricing can actually work

Prescriptive optimisation, in a regulated vertical, with a documented baseline. Direct KPI attribution, scarce competing skills, no category branding to fight through. Not agentic AI, where the whole market is pointing and where attribution collapses under audit.

C6 · Large Deal Skills

The roles that gate participation in end-to-end deals, and none of them are technical.

Theme One needs ontologists and optimisation engineers. Theme Two needs evaluation and retrieval engineers. This theme needs something different: people who can hold a multi-tower shape, absorb headcount, and price risk. That is why large-deal capability cannot be built by hiring more engineers.

Why the FDE model connects everything

A forward deployed engineer scopes the use case, designs the architecture, writes production code, and stays on the account through renewal, paid for outcomes rather than hours. That is the same shape as attaching to a prime's programme and pricing on output units. Every major ecosystem now runs an FDE or forward-deployed-expert programme: OpenAI's pilot, Anthropic's applied group, Google Cloud embedding its own alongside seven named integrators, and Cognizant supplying its own into Gemini Enterprise deployments. HCLTech is the only Indian services firm named in retrieved reporting as building an FDE cadre.

The uncomfortable implication

FDE economics require a flat, senior, expensive team billing less volume at higher value. That is structurally incompatible with leverage staffing, and it is a P&L change rather than a training programme. It is the hardest thing in this analysis for an established services firm to execute, and it is why most will talk about FDEs without building them.

C8 · Client Satisfaction and Engagement Quality

A dimension absent from every prior version of this analysis, and the one buyers actually act on.

Capability, IP and partner standing describe what a firm can do. None of them describe whether clients want to keep working with it. That gap ran through this whole analysis unexamined until it was pointed out, and it matters because renewal and reference are the two things that convert a workstream into a programme.

The credible public source

Whitelane Research is the most useful independent instrument found. The 2025/26 Financial Services Europe study surveyed close to 600 participants from major IT-spending financial services organisations, evaluating more than 1,500 individual sourcing relationships on service delivery quality, account management, pricing, innovation and transformation capability. The Nordics 2026 study gathered over 450 participants across more than 1,000 relationships, scored on service delivery, value for money and security.

It measures the relationship, at relationship level, from the buyer side. That is a different and in some ways harder currency than an analyst capability placement, because it cannot be influenced by a vendor briefing.

What it shows

Infosys ranked first for Financial Services Banking in Europe with an 83% general satisfaction score, improving three percentage points year on year, and first in Financial Services in the Nordics, with top-three Exceptional Performer placings in Financial Services Overall and in Cloud and Infrastructure Services. That is a client-side result, not a capability assessment, and it is the strongest satisfaction evidence retrieved for any firm in this dataset.

The honest limitation, which is severe

Firm-level NPS is essentially never disclosed by Indian IT services firms. No published NPS was found for any of the 62 firms here. What exists publicly is generic industry benchmarking — that above 0 is positive, above 20 favourable, above 50 excellent — which tells you nothing about any specific competitor. Whitelane satisfaction scores are the closest usable proxy and they are relationship-level and geography-scoped, not data and AI specific. HFS Horizons ran a Customer Experience Services assessment across 33 providers in 2026, but that assesses CX delivery capability rather than the provider’s own client satisfaction.

What to do with the gap

Three moves. Use Whitelane placements as competitive intelligence in European financial services pursuits, where they are directly citable. Recognise that your own client satisfaction data is proprietary evidence competitors cannot match publicly — which makes it a stronger differentiator than any capability claim precisely because the public record is empty. And when a competitor cites an analyst placement, the counter-question is whether they can produce relationship-level buyer-side satisfaction data, because almost none can.

Not researched

Renewal rates, account expansion, delivery escalation frequency and client concentration are not covered anywhere in this analysis. None are reliably public. They are also the metrics that most directly predict whether a firm holds a large deal once it wins one.

Known gaps, stated plainly

There was no sampling frame, and that is the most serious methodological failure here. The original 38 firms accumulated from search results plus recall rather than from a constructed universe, which is convenience sampling presented as a dataset. Twenty-four firms were added afterwards to close the most significant omissions, including EXL, named top Leader in the Everest Group Data and AI Services Specialists PEAK Matrix for two consecutive years and now the firm with the most shipped assets anywhere in this dataset. Missing that on the first pass is not a marginal gap. Sonata Software was visible in a source already read and omitted anyway. Treat the current 62 as a working frame that remains incomplete rather than as the market.

Roughly 77% of capability cells are unchecked, concentrated in the firms added late and in private boutiques. Unchecked is rendered as a distinct visual state precisely so it cannot be read as absence. A firm with an all-blank row has not been assessed and no conclusion should be drawn from it.

Component traction is a proxy, not a measurement. The stack analysis is built from platform vendor roadmaps and launch announcements, which show where vendors are investing. No independent adoption measurement per component was found for any platform.

Nineteen of the twenty-four added firms are name-only rows. Only EXL, Axtria, ITC Infotech and, partially, Genpact and WNS were researched. Mu Sigma, Saama, Course5, Affine, Sigmoid, Gramener, Infocepts, Altimetrik, Infogain, Encora, Thoughtworks, LTTS, KPIT, Cyient, Datamatics, eClerx, ZS Associates, Virtusa and Sonata Software are in the frame but not assessed. Adding a name is not the same as adding a data point, and their blank rows should be read as unexamined rather than as evidence of absence.

No deal-level analysis was done for any added firm, and no wins, accelerator inventories or customer engagements were remapped for them. The density charts and coverage statistics recalculate automatically against 62, but the qualitative findings were written against 38 and only the load-bearing claims have been restated.

Under-researched relative to the rest: Microsoft's partner mechanics and Azure AI Foundry, Meta's enterprise channel, AWS competency requirements beyond the AI categories, and telecom and manufacturing verticals. These are flagged rather than filled, and should not be compared on equal footing with Databricks, Anthropic or Google Cloud, which were researched in depth.

What a shipped mark does not prove: that the asset is good, that claimed automation percentages are real, or that it would survive technical evaluation. Nothing here has been tested against a working system. Partner tier data is a point-in-time snapshot; tiers change quarterly, Databricks renamed its ladder mid-window, and both model-vendor programmes are months old.

D1 · Correction, Then Method

The portfolio walkthrough changed the diagnosis. Two of my findings were wrong.

Seeing the Orian stack overturns two things I asserted. I scored Xoriant’s semantic capability as absent; it is not. Data Ontology, the Context Engineering Layer and Hybrid RAG with knowledge graphs are three named, described semantic assets, which places Xoriant in a cohort of four across 63 firms rather than outside it. And I stated HCLTech was the only Indian services firm building a forward deployed engineer cadre; Orian Houses is a productised talent architecture with separate FDE and PDE tracks, a structured curriculum and externally portable certifications, which is more developed than the HCLTech reporting describes.

What the portal actually is, and why my earlier criticism was wrong

I described the ORIAN site returning nothing to a crawler as a failure. It is not. The portal is authenticated and carries House and Customer Success Unit spokesperson directories with individual employee contacts. It is an internal enablement and discovery tool, and it appears to do that job well — portfolio search, tagged assets by persona and function, named owners per house.

The real finding is different and narrower: there is no market-facing counterpart. Roughly thirty assets already have written descriptions. None of them exist on a page a buyer, analyst or platform vendor can reach.

The method, unchanged

Every other firm was scored on public evidence only. That standard is kept, but Xoriant is now scored twice: public evidence, the column comparable to everyone else, and actual, based on the portfolio walkthrough. The distance between those two columns is the finding, and it is not a small one.

The accidental test still stands. Across roughly fifteen landscape searches run before Xoriant was in scope, it never surfaced once. Every firm in the top two evidence tiers appeared organically at least once. That measures visibility, not capability, and the two turn out to be very far apart here.

The shape of it

The profile is unusual and, read correctly, encouraging. D4 · Positioning and Ranking

Where Xoriant sits on public evidence today, and what moves it.

Four tiers, defined by what a buyer can verify rather than by revenue or headcount. The tier boundaries are evidence thresholds, not quality judgements — several Tier 4 firms are probably stronger than the tier implies and simply have not been assessed.

What moves Xoriant from Tier 3 to Tier 2

One externally verifiable credential. Not a portfolio of them — one. A Databricks Brickbuilder specialization, a cloud marketplace listing, an Anthropic Services Track Select placement, or inclusion in one ISG Provider Lens cohort would each do it independently. The tier boundary between 3 and 2 is a single artifact, which is why this is the highest-return work available.

What moves Tier 2 to Tier 1

Multiple shipped assets plus an earned tier plus independent recognition, sustained rather than one-off. Award repetition is what separates the top of this dataset: Tredence won Databricks Retail and CPG four consecutive years, EXL was top Everest Leader twice running. That is a two to three year programme, not a campaign, and it should not be the near-term target.

D6 · The Asset Being Under-Used

Quantified regulated BFSI delivery is the strongest public evidence Xoriant has, and it is buried.

What is already published

Basel III compliance: GenAI automating over 7,000 rules, standardising reporting and minimising penalty risk across jurisdictions for a global bank. Bulgaria Euro adoption: enterprise-wide currency conversion for a global bank against a legally mandated 1 January 2026 deadline. Fenergo alliance: KYC and CLM process acceleration with regulatory compliance framing.

These are quantified, deadline-bound, regulator-driven engagements. Very few mid-tier competitors can show anything comparable, and none of the Tier 3 peer group can.

Why it is under-used

It sits in a case-study carousel rather than being the spine of the positioning. A buyer arriving at the site sees generic digital engineering language first — product engineering DNA, AI-native platforms, measurable outcomes — and the regulated proof second, if at all. The undifferentiated claim is doing the work that the differentiated evidence should be doing.

The connection to the semantic thesis

This is where the two halves join. A bank automating 7,000+ Basel III rules is, functionally, an exercise in encoding regulatory meaning into machine-executable form. That is the same problem as standards-grounded ontology, approached from the delivery end rather than the architecture end.

The credible version of the semantic claim is therefore not "we have a semantic layer" — which three competitors already say and which the production data suggests is hard. It is "we have encoded regulatory meaning at scale in production, under a legally mandated deadline, and here is the number." That is a harder claim to copy and it is already true.

The test before committing

Whether the Basel III client will be named publicly. An unnamed case study is Grade B evidence; a named one with a client quote is Grade A and clears the reference requirement for Anthropic Select simultaneously. Start that conversation before anything else on this tab, because it has the longest lead time and unblocks the most.
